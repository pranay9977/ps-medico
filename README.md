1. PS_Medico.html - the offline app. Open it in any modern browser (Chrome/Edge/Safari/Firefox, double-click the file). Type ANY medicine name of ANY company (e.g. Dolo 650, Zayo 50 or Sinarest New) and it shows the salt combination, YOUR pack's size, price and per-tablet price, what it is used for, common side effects, and the same-salt alternatives from 25 leading Indian and global makers - so you can compare your medicine's price with the alternatives' prices directly. Every opened medicine also shows its MAKER'S TRUST SCORE out of 100 - built only from public regulatory records (US FDA warning letters, import alerts, recalls, company type and market footprint; no paid rankings, basis written under every bar). 57 major makers are rated; small makers without any public record honestly show "not rated" instead of an invented number. The MAIN TABLE lists only packs with the same salts AND the same mg strength as the medicine you searched ("100% same salts and same strength") - the direct substitutes. Packs with the same salts at other strengths (e.g. 50mg vs 100mg) are a different dose and sit in their own collapsed "different strength" section below, so they never mix into the main list. Each row shows Product, Company (with FDA status and trust bar in the row details), Salt combination, Pack, Price (MRP snapshot) and "Per unit" (pack price divided by the pieces in the pack) - a "best value" tag marks the cheapest pack per tablet/capsule, which can be a bigger bottle, and "lowest price" marks the cheapest pack MRP. CLICK ANY ROW to open its full details - everything the app knows about that pack: product, company with FDA import status and trust score, complete salt combination with strengths, pack, price, per-unit price, what it is used for, and common side effects (click again to close). Clicking a SALT opens that salt's own page (uses, common side effects, every maker that sells it, all its packs, and how many of the 253,809 catalog listings contain it). Clicking a COMPANY name (in any table, row details or match card) opens the maker's page - FDA status, the full public-record basis of its trust score, and all its products; smaller makers not followed in detail honestly show "not rated" together with their medicines from the national catalog. SYMPTOM SEARCH: type a complaint in plain words (fever, headache, acidity, cough, constipation...) and the app lists the salts whose catalog use texts mention it, plus a second card with salts whose listed side effects mention your word - a lookup, not a prescription; ask your doctor. Nothing is cut: when a table shows only the first rows (e.g. first 200), a "Show all" button under it expands the complete list. If your pack is marked discontinued in the catalog, a warning appears on its card. The famous brand for that salt (e.g. Crocin for paracetamol) is starred and listed first on a gold row - in the different-strength section if its strength differs from yours. Every medicine page also carries the "Spot it in the shop" diagram - the six items printed on every legal strip (brand, salts + mg, maker, printed MRP, batch/date, licence no.), which identifies a pack in the shop better than a photo; the app deliberately shows no pack photos, because a wrong photo is more dangerous than none. Spelling help: when nothing is found, the app suggests similarly spelled brands ("did you mean...") - e.g. "rozufirst" finds Rozfirst, and "rozugold" finds Rozagold (Sun Pharma, one of the leading makers); you can also type the salt names from your strip (e.g. "rosuvastatin") directly in the search box, or type a symptom (e.g. "fever"). COVERAGE: all 253,809 medicine listings of India's largest open medicine catalog are inside - every medicine AND every pack size, nothing removed (verified row by row: 0 missing) - covering every state including MP, UP, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Delhi/NCR and Chhattisgarh, because Indian medicine brands are national (many are literally made in Madhya Pradesh's own Pithampur-Indore pharma hub by Cipla, Sun, Lupin, Intas, Aurobindo, Alembic, Glenmark, Macleods etc., all of which are in the app). When none of the leading makers combines all your salts in one medicine, a "missing one salt" section lists the closest brands, with the missing salt written on each row - not the same medicine; ask your doctor. Layout: the medicine list starts DIRECTLY under the search box. The company on/off picker (quick filters + 25 maker chips) is hidden behind the small "&#9881;&#65039; Filters 25/25" button next to the search box - click it to open or close it; the number shows how many makers are switched on, and the button turns amber when not all 25 are on. While you scroll a long list, the sticky header shrinks to just the search bar and grows back when you return to the top. Matching medicines appear as compact cards in two to three columns on a computer and one column on a phone. Dark mode: the moon/sun button next to the search box switches dark mode on/off, and the choice is remembered on this device. Sharing: send the file itself (about 9 MB) by WhatsApp, email or USB - it needs no internet, no installation and no account, and each device keeps its own recent searches. Your last 8 searched medicines appear as "Recent" chips under the search bar (saved on this device only; click one to reopen it instantly). The "Build a combination" button lets you pick the salts (with mg strengths) yourself - every salt sold in India is in the list. A "Print / save PDF" button prints just the results (collapsed sections open automatically when printing; printing always stays on a white background), and the FAQ at the bottom explains every tag, the trust-score rubric, why one company sells so many different tablets (different strengths and pack sizes - no salts are hidden), and what is included in the catalog. The page carries no visible version clutter (clean for sharing) - instead, the main page and footer show the credit "An easy help by PS Choudhary" (in Hindi mode: "PS Choudhary द्वारा एक सरल सहायता").
